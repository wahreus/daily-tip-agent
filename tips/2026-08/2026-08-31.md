## Tip of the day [Monday, August 31, 2026]

### Prefer short-lived access over stored keys

Replace long-term access keys with temporary credentials wherever possible, especially for human users and routine workload access. For workforce identities, use federation through a centralized identity provider or AWS IAM Identity Center so people authenticate once and receive short-lived credentials instead of keeping IAM users and static keys. For applications and scripts, use IAM roles to obtain temporary credentials rather than hard-coding secret access keys. If you have on-premises or external systems, consider IAM Roles Anywhere or other AWS-supported credential providers that avoid storing long-term AWS credentials. Where temporary credentials are not supported, keep the remaining long-term keys tightly scoped and rotate them regularly.

**Why it matters:** Short-lived credentials reduce the blast radius of a leaked secret because they expire quickly and do not need to be stored for long periods. They also lower operational risk by eliminating many manual rotation and distribution tasks, while aligning access more closely with least privilege.
