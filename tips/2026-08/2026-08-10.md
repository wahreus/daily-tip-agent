## Tip of the day [Monday, August 10, 2026]

### Define, replicate, and test for the recovery objective you actually need

Build your disaster recovery plan around explicit RTO and RPO targets, then choose a recovery strategy that can realistically meet them. For many workloads, a warm standby or pilot light setup in a secondary AWS Region is a practical balance between cost and resilience, especially when you continuously replicate data and keep baseline infrastructure ready. Make sure your plan also protects against data corruption by using versioning or point-in-time recovery, not just replicas. Back up the replicated data in the recovery site as well, so you have a path to restore clean copies if the replicas themselves are compromised. Most importantly, regularly exercise failover and recovery procedures to verify that the workload can meet its objectives when a real incident happens.

**Why it matters:** A DR plan that is only documented, but never tested, often fails when needed most. Regular validation reduces the risk of missed dependencies, reveals gaps in recovery tooling, and gives your team confidence that recovery objectives are achievable.
