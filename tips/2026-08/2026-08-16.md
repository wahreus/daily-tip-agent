## Tip of the day [Sunday, August 16, 2026]

### Keep data close to where it is used

Design your workload so common data is accessed through shared file systems or object storage rather than copied repeatedly between services. Before moving data, reduce its size and format it efficiently, because moving less data lowers network usage and related costs. If your application serves users in different places, place data closer to the consumers instead of keeping everything in one Region. For workloads that exchange large volumes of data, consider compression, caching, and edge delivery to avoid unnecessary back-and-forth transfers. Also watch for cross-AZ and cross-Region movement, since those paths often add avoidable cost and complexity. Regularly review network flows to find places where data can be filtered, shared, or localized instead of moved.

**Why it matters:** Unnecessary data movement consumes network capacity and can increase latency, cost, and operational overhead. Reducing transfers also lowers the environmental impact of your workload and often improves performance by keeping compute and data closer together.
