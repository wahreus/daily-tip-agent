## Tip of the day [Sunday, August 30, 2026]

### Automate retention, transition, and deletion

Treat data lifecycle management as a set of automated rules, not a manual cleanup task. Start by classifying data as early as possible, then define how long each data type should be retained, when it should move to colder or more energy-efficient storage, and when it must be destroyed. Use service-specific lifecycle controls where possible, such as snapshot retention for EBS, lifecycle management for EFS, and image expiration policies for ECR. Align these policies with legal, regulatory, and organizational requirements so that deletion is deliberate and auditable. Continuously monitor for resources that do not have lifecycle automation enabled and adjust policies as workload needs change.

**Why it matters:** Clear lifecycle policies reduce storage cost, improve operational efficiency, and lower the risk of retaining data longer than allowed or useful. They also strengthen compliance and security by making retention and disposal consistent, tracked, and enforceable.
