## Tip of the day [Monday, August 17, 2026]

### Encrypt every path, not just the edge

Treat encryption in transit as a requirement for both internet-facing and internal service-to-service traffic. Use only modern TLS, ideally TLS 1.3 where possible, and disable deprecated protocols and weak cipher suites. For public endpoints, enforce HTTPS and block any HTTP access so clients cannot accidentally fall back to cleartext. For internal calls, don’t rely on the network boundary alone; authenticate and authorize traffic between services as well, using mechanisms like mTLS or signed requests where appropriate. Put certificate renewal on a schedule or automate it so expiring X.509 certificates do not become an outage risk. If you terminate TLS at a load balancer or edge service, make sure the connection from that point onward is still protected according to your security requirements.

**Why it matters:** Encryption in transit reduces the chance of interception or tampering as data moves between clients, services, and AWS APIs. Strong protocol enforcement and certificate hygiene also prevent avoidable security gaps and reliability issues caused by expired or misconfigured certificates.
