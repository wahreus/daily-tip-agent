## Tip of the day [Wed, September 23, 2026]

### Design autoscaling to match demand and recover quickly

For demand-based scaling, pick an automatic scaling mechanism that matches the workload component: EC2 Auto Scaling for instances, Application Auto Scaling for managed services, and HPA or KEDA for Kubernetes workloads. Use target tracking when you want the system to continuously hold a metric near a defined target, and consider step scaling when you need explicit, metric-based adjustments. Make sure you understand how fast new capacity must come online, because the gap between supply and demand can change quickly during spikes. Test both scale-out and scale-in behavior so you do not leave excess capacity running after traffic drops. Also verify your minimum and maximum bounds so the workload can scale enough to handle peak load without overshooting cost or quota limits.

**Why it matters:** Good demand-based scaling keeps performance steady during spikes while reducing waste when demand falls. It also helps you avoid the common anti-patterns of manual scaling and leaving extra capacity in place after a surge.
