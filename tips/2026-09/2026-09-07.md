## Tip of the day [Monday, September 7, 2026]

### Match the database to the data, not the other way around

Start by documenting the workload’s data type, access patterns, growth rate, and durability needs before choosing a database. If your workload needs foreign key integrity or ACID transactions, a relational database such as Amazon RDS or Aurora is a strong fit. If the data is key-value or document-oriented and you want to avoid joins, a NoSQL design like DynamoDB can be a better match, especially when you can model the data for single-request access. For workloads that need strong consistency in NoSQL, use strongly consistent reads where supported rather than forcing the wrong database type. Avoid standardizing on one database just because the team knows it well; purpose-built data stores usually deliver better performance and simpler operations.

**Why it matters:** Choosing based on workload characteristics reduces rework, improves performance, and helps you avoid hidden scaling or consistency problems later. It also keeps operational overhead aligned with the actual needs of the application instead of the familiarity of the team.
