## Tip of the day [Saturday, September 26, 2026]

### Make improvement a scheduled operational habit

Treat continuous operational improvement as a routine part of your operating model, not a side project. Set aside dedicated time and resources each sprint or cycle to make small, incremental changes to your runbooks, automation, alerts, and deployment processes. Use safe environments such as sandboxes, dev, and test to try changes before promoting them, and increase controls as you move closer to production. Pair every improvement with a clear success criterion so you can evaluate whether it actually made operations more effective or efficient. When a change does not meet the goal, keep the feedback loop active and try an alternative approach rather than stopping the effort. Capture lessons from incidents and game days, then share them broadly so other teams can benefit from the same operational learning.

**Why it matters:** Continuous improvement works best when it is deliberate, repeatable, and low risk. Structured feedback loops and small experiments help teams learn faster, reduce operational friction, and steadily improve reliability and delivery speed.
