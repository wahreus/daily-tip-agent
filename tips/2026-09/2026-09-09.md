## Tip of the day [Wed, September 9, 2026]

### Throttle on retry, buffer on no-retry

If your clients can retry safely, use throttling to slow excess traffic instead of letting a spike overwhelm the service. If clients cannot retry, put a buffer such as a queue in front of the work so requests can be processed later without dropping them. Size both the throttle and the buffer from real demand data: expected peak rate, rate of change, request size, and the response time you must preserve. Make sure your design accounts for duplicate requests when buffering is used, because retries and deferred processing can reintroduce the same work more than once. Load test both the maximum request rate and the maximum request size together, and verify the throttling limits match what you actually provisioned. For API-based workloads, consider service-level throttles and per-consumer limits so a single noisy client cannot consume all capacity.

**Why it matters:** Throttling and load shedding are most effective when they are intentional, measurable, and aligned with client behavior. Done well, they protect the workload from retry storms and traffic spikes while preserving service for supported requests.
