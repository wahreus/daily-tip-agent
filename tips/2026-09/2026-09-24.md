## Tip of the day [Thursday, September 24, 2026]

### Prefer asynchronous boundaries for unstable dependencies

Design component interactions so services depend on versioned, published interfaces instead of direct, tightly chained calls. Where an immediate response is not required, use asynchronous patterns such as queues, streams, workflows, or event buses to decouple producers from consumers. This lets one component evolve or fail without forcing every downstream system to fail at the same time. Avoid long synchronous chains and make sure your retry and error-handling logic is intentional, especially when a dependency is unhealthy or unavailable. If the work can tolerate a brief delay, acknowledge receipt first and process it later rather than blocking the caller. That simple shift usually makes systems easier to scale, test, and change safely.

**Why it matters:** Loose coupling reduces the blast radius of failures and makes it easier to isolate, diagnose, and recover from issues. It also gives teams more freedom to ship changes independently without coordinating every deployment across the entire chain.
