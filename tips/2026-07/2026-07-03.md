## Tip of the day [Friday, July 3, 2026]

### Treat change management as a controlled rollback-ready process

Plan for unsuccessful changes before you ship them by defining how you will detect, revert, and document a bad deployment. Use automation for the full deployment pipeline so testing, promotion, and rollback happen consistently instead of relying on manual steps or emergency workarounds. Prefer safe rollout patterns such as canary, rolling, blue/green, or traffic splitting so any defect is limited to a small slice of traffic first. Make sure every change stays within governance requirements and that you have a clear baseline for expected resource utilization before release. Avoid skipping bake time or rushing follow-on deployments, because that often hides issues until they affect more users. A simple pre-mortem before release can help the team validate that the rollback path and failure handling are actually usable.

**Why it matters:** Controlled change management reduces human error and limits the blast radius when something goes wrong. It also improves compliance and makes it faster to recover to a known-good state without turning every incident into an emergency release.
