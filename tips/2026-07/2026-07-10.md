## Tip of the day [Friday, July 10, 2026]

### Choose the database that matches your data model and access pattern

Start by defining whether your workload needs ACID transactions, referential integrity, strong consistency, or mostly key-value access. If you need foreign keys and complex joins, a relational database like Amazon RDS or Aurora is usually the safer choice. If your application is driven by predictable access patterns and high-throughput reads or writes, a purpose-built NoSQL store such as DynamoDB can fit better. For read-heavy relational workloads, consider read replicas or caching to reduce pressure on the primary database. Avoid choosing a database just because your team already knows it well; select it based on data characteristics, latency, scalability, and operational needs.

**Why it matters:** The wrong database choice often creates unnecessary complexity, performance bottlenecks, and higher operational overhead. Matching the engine to the workload helps you scale more cleanly and keep the system easier to operate and evolve.
