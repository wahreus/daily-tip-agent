## Tip of the day [Friday, October 2, 2026]

### Predefine and practice your incident response playbook

Build a written incident response plan before you need it, and treat it as a living document that you regularly update and test. Define clear roles and responsibilities across security, operations, legal, leadership, and application SMEs so everyone knows who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed during an event. Pre-provision the tooling and access needed for investigation, containment, and recovery, including logging, automated response actions, and a known-good recovery path. Then run game days or incident simulations to verify that your team can isolate affected systems quickly and restore service without improvising under pressure. If your environment includes multiple workloads, make sure the plan accounts for how to contain an incident without slowing down recovery for unaffected services.

**Why it matters:** Well-prepared teams respond faster, reduce blast radius, and recover with less guesswork during a real security event. A practiced plan also improves coordination across technical and non-technical stakeholders, which is critical when decisions need to be made quickly.
