## Tip of the day [Saturday, October 3, 2026]

### Use target tracking with tested thresholds

For demand-based scaling, prefer a target-tracking policy that automatically adds or removes capacity to keep a key metric near a chosen target. Pick a metric that reflects real user demand, such as request rate, queue depth, or CPU for the specific component, and validate the target with load testing before you rely on it. Set sensible minimum and maximum limits so the system can scale up enough for spikes but still scale back down when demand drops. If your workload has predictable patterns, combine demand-based scaling with predictive or scheduled scaling for known busy periods. Make sure the application can handle both scale-out and scale-in events without losing requests or leaving excess capacity behind.

**Why it matters:** This approach helps match supply to demand automatically, which improves performance during peaks and avoids paying for idle resources afterward. It also reduces the operational burden of manual scaling while making the workload more resilient to changing traffic.
